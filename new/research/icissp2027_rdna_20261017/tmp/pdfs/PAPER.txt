    Availability Claims Need an Evidence Contract: Auditing Preﬁx-Cache
                                  Isolation

                                               Anonymous Submission



Keywords:      LLM Serving, Availability, Preﬁx Caching, Isolation, Experimental Methodology.

Abstract:      Preﬁx-cache namespace isolation restricts reuse across principals, while the requests can still share a ﬁnite
               KV block pool and scheduling budget. Existing research already establishes both cache-induced unfairness
               and resource-exhaustion attacks. The remaining evaluation question is what an experiment can identify when
               the isolation policy itself changes executed work and memory occupancy. We argue for a claim-speciﬁc
               evidence contract that separates total deployment-policy cost, a preﬁx-speciﬁc control contrast, and attribution
               to a resource mechanism. Complete outcome measurements must remain visible when mechanism checks
               abstain. We instantiate this position through a source audit of vLLM 0.17.1 and an independent reconstruction
               of 14 existing exploratory episodes containing 25 requests and 1,202 streaming events. Two episodes retain
               complete victim measurements but fail the original mechanism-attribution checks after preemption. A source-
               calibrated counterexample also shows why logged generation tokens cannot be equated with newly emitted
               tokens. These observations motivate the contract; they do not establish a new attack or causal interaction. We
               specify controls, counterexamples, falsiﬁcation criteria, and a bounded validation study.
