# NordSec — proposed compact manuscript supplementation (exploratory, retrospective)

## Experimental validation: the temporal redistribution of victim interference

We further analyzed the raw client-side SSE receipts of the completed 32-episode validation campaign, comparing `max_num_batched_tokens` values of 512 and 2048. For each budget, eight paired burst/no-burst blocks contained the same eight preselected decoding victims. Within a block, we subtracted each victim's trigger-relative token receipt time in the no-burst episode from the corresponding receipt time in the burst episode, then averaged over the eight victims. Blocks, not requests or tokens, served as bootstrap units; 95% intervals are descriptive given the eight paired blocks per budget.

At token 32, the average additional delay was **1,294 ms** for budget 512 versus **1,728 ms** for budget 2048 (paired across-budget difference: **433 ms**, 95% bootstrap CI approximately **411–455 ms**). At token 64, the corresponding delays were **1,756 ms** and **1,744 ms** (contrast **−11 ms**, 95% CI approximately **−31 to +8 ms**). The additional delay accumulated specifically *between* token positions 32 and 64 was **461 ms** at budget 512 and **16 ms** at budget 2048 (difference **−445 ms**, 95% CI approximately **−458 to −432 ms**). Thus, under this fixed workload, smaller scheduling-token budgets changed the temporal location of cross-request interference, with a marked reduction in early token delay but no demonstrated reduction in the additional delay through token 64. The latter observation is not an equivalence test. Scheduler logs corroborate distinct prefill chunking, but do not identify an individual GPU-side cause for each client-visible pause.

## Offered-load-normalized external delay

Each burst offers 8,192 input tokens and 64 generated output tokens. Summing the eight paired increases in client-visible time to token 64 yields **14.04 victim-endpoint seconds** per burst under budget 512 (95% CI **13.95–14.14 s**) and **13.95 s** under budget 2048 (95% CI **13.86–14.05 s**). Dividing these values by the offered input-token budget gives **1.714** and **1.703 victim-endpoint seconds per 1,000 offered input tokens**, respectively. These are transparent *external endpoint-delay normalizations*, not estimates of GPU busy time, service capacity loss, attack cost, or achievable adversarial synchronization in deployment.

## Sensitivity to published token deadlines

Using the three application-dependent decode targets of **25, 50 and 100 ms** described in Etalon (Agrawal et al., 2024, §4.1), we compared the fraction of client-observed inter-token intervals ending after the trigger that exceeded each threshold. Relative to paired no-burst controls, the added fraction (percentage points) for budget 512 was **39.43 / 36.48 / 34.33**, versus **11.22 / 8.35 / 8.35** for budget 2048. Thus, the smaller budget reduced the worst individual stall but caused more above-target inter-token intervals, indicating a frequency–severity trade-off in this controlled interference setting. This direct threshold analysis does **not** implement Etalon's complete fluidity-index: a calibrated prompt-length-specific prefill deadline was unavailable, and simple exceedance counts do not account for its slack and deadline-reset rules. An exploratory decode-only replay using these rules showed sensitivity to the target and the definition of the first post-trigger interval; consequently, no overall Etalon superiority claim follows.

All comparisons are observational at the client's SSE-receipt boundary. The 22 victim pairs with differing generated token identities remain included, so progress is compared at ordinal output positions rather than identical textual completions. The prior, non-robust aggregate offload-by-burst primary result is unchanged; these analyses are supplementary and exploratory.
